RESERVE BANK OF INDIA - MASTER DIRECTIONS ON CYBER RESILIENCE AND DIGITAL PAYMENT SECURITY CONTROLS FOR NON-BANK PAYMENT SYSTEM OPERATORS (PSOs)
Circular No: RBI/DPSS/2024-25/123 | CO.DPSS.OVRST.No.S447/06-26-002/2024-25 | July 30, 2024
Issued under Section 10(2) read with Section 18 of Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (Act 51 of 2007).

SECTION I – PRELIMINARY
1. Introduction: Issued in public interest to ensure cyber resilience and secure digital payment systems.
2. Short Title: RBI (Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank PSOs) Master Directions, 2024.
3. Phased Implementation Timeline:
   - Large non-bank PSOs (CCIL, NPCI, NPBL, Card Networks, ATM Networks, WLAOs, Large PPIs, TReDS, BBPOUs, Payment Aggregators): April 1, 2025.
   - Medium non-bank PSOs (Cross-border MTSS, Medium PPIs): April 1, 2026.
   - Small non-bank PSOs (Small PPIs, Instant Money Transfer): April 1, 2028.
4. Applicability: All authorised non-bank PSOs.
5. Ecosystem Adherence: PSOs shall ensure adherence by unregulated entities in digital payment ecosystem (payment gateways, third-party service providers, vendors) via mutual agreements and Board-approved policy.
6. Purpose: Framework for information security preparedness with emphasis on cyber resilience.

SECTION II – GOVERNANCE CONTROLS
7. Board Oversight: Board responsible for info security & cyber resilience; delegation to Board sub-committee headed by cybersecurity expert, meeting at least once every quarter.
8. Information Security (IS) Policy: Formulate Board-approved IS policy reviewed annually covering roles, cyber risk assessment/management, controls, training.
9. Cyber Crisis Management Plan (CCMP): Distinct Board-approved CCMP to detect, contain, respond, and recover from cyber attacks (aligned with CERT-In, NCIIPC, IDRBT).
10. Chief Information Security Officer (CISO): Accountability entrusted to senior executive (CISO) reporting directly to top management.
11. KRIs & KPIs: Define Key Risk Indicators to identify risk events and Key Performance Indicators to assess control effectiveness; continuous sub-committee monitoring.
12. IT Assessment Reports: Place system audit and VA/PT reports before IT oversight sub-committee in meeting immediately following report availability.
13. Cyber Risk Assessment for Changes: Cyber risk assessment before launching new products, services, technologies, or major infrastructure changes under CISO oversight.

SECTION III – BASELINE INFORMATION SECURITY MEASURES / CONTROLS
14. Inventory Management:
   (a) Record and classify all roles, assets (apps, data, infra, personnel), critical functions, third parties with criticality & business value.
   (b) Process flow diagram of network resources, inter-connections, dependencies, and data flows.
   (c) Asset details: identifier, network address, location, owner, End-of-Life Support (EoLS).
15. Identity and Access Management (IAM):
   (a) Policies and controls for access privileges and administration.
   (b) Digital identity for all individuals with IT access, monitored till termination.
   (c) Default authentication settings deactivated and changed prior to rollout.
   (d) Access based on need-to-have, need-to-know, and Principle of Least Privilege.
   (e) Privileged accounts require Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), tight monitoring, and rotation policy.
   (f) Centralized whitelist/blacklist for removable media and portable devices (laptops, smartphones).
   (g) Remote/WFH access requires adequate precautions including MFA.
   (h) Automatic limit, lock, and termination of system and remote sessions after pre-defined inactivity.
   (i) Physical and environmental safeguards with periodic testing against disasters and physical threats.
16. Network Security:
   (a) Network devices configured and checked periodically for security rules.
   (b) 24x7x365 Security Operations Centre (SOC) for centralized log monitoring, detection, escalation, and quick response.
   (c) Automated SIEM correlating network/system alerts across business units to detect multi-faceted attacks.
   (d) Anti-malware scanning all incoming data to prevent installation/infection.
   (e) Multi-layered boundary defences monitoring network traffic and filtering ingress/egress data.
   (f) Network segmentation based on role, location, environment (prod, test, dev); application & port whitelisting.
   (g) Connect devices (laptops, desktops, mobiles) to network only after verifying compliance with security requirements.
17. Application Security Life Cycle (ASLC):
   (a) 'Secure by design' S-SDLC preventing security weaknesses during build.
   (b) Multi-tier architecture segregating database layer from other layers for digital payment products.
   (c) Source code acquisition or escrow arrangement for critical third-party applications to ensure continuity.
18. Security Testing:
   (a) Rigorous security testing (source code review, VA, PT) by qualified professionals at least annually in authenticated mode.
   (b) Developer certificate of no vulnerabilities/malware/covert channels for third-party software; fresh cert on code changes.
   (c) Time-bound remediation of deficiencies; report recurring issues to Board IT sub-committee with analysis.
   (d) Pre-deployment security audits (VA/PT) before deploying or redeploying critical services and components.
19. Vendor Risk Management:
   (a) Security controls preventing infiltration into PSO network from vendor environments.
   (b) Adhere to legal/regulatory requirements on geographical location of infrastructure and data storage (Data Localization), ensuring vendor compliance.
   (c) Independent auditor certified assurance on cyber resilience for critical vendors.
20. Data Security:
   (a) Comprehensive Data Leak Prevention (DLP) policy for business and customer information (in transit and at rest).
   (b) Mechanisms for traceability and visibility of data assets.
   (c) Information Security Management System (ISMS) implementation based on standards (ISO 27001).
   (d) Secure handling, processing, storage of PII; data-in-transit and data-at-rest encryption (data or channel encryption).
   (e) Adhere to PCI-DSS guidelines and obtain PCI-DSS certification for card data storage.
   (f) Half-yearly testing of backed-up data to ensure recovery without loss of transactions or audit trails.
21. Patch and Change Management:
   (a) Documented policy to identify and apply OEM patches.
   (b) Immediate application of critical patches for well-known/reported attacks.
   (c) Robust change management preserving overall IT setup integrity.
   (d) Patches and changes tested in non-production before production implementation.
22. Incident Response:
   (a) Board-approved incident response mechanism with prompt notification to senior management, employees, regulators.
   (b) Readiness for incident scenarios based on situational awareness and coordination.
   (c) Post-incident forensic analysis and Root Cause Analysis (RCA) to avoid recurrence.
   (d) Mandatory 6-hour incident reporting to RBI (Incident Reporting Format Annex 1) and CERT-In for cyber-attacks, outages, fraud, settlement delay.
23. Business Continuity Plan (BCP):
   (a) BCP based on cyber threat scenarios reviewed annually, including cyber incident response, resumption, and recovery.
   (b) Safe resumption of critical operations aligned with Recovery Time Objective (RTO) and Recovery Point Objective (RPO).
   (c) Strive to achieve near-zero RPO.
   (d) Disaster Recovery (DR) facility located in a different seismic zone than Primary Data Centre (PDC) with defined data reconciliation.
   (e) Half-yearly DR drills; urgent rectification of any RTO/RPO divergence.
24. Application Programming Interfaces (APIs):
   (a) Safeguards for APIs: Authentication and Authorization, Message Content Confidentiality, Resource Integrity, Availability & Threat Protection (anomaly identification).
   (b) Adherence to relevant standards and globally recognized API security frameworks (OWASP API Top 10).
25. Employee Awareness & Training:
   (a) Periodic repeated training on info security for employees and vendors.
   (b) Periodic evaluation of awareness; employees below benchmark score restricted/prohibited from accessing information assets.
   (c) Board members and key senior management sensitized on info security and cyber risks.
26. Cloud Security:
   (a) Board-approved cloud operations policy covering cloud activities, CSP roles, data localisation, protection, and recoverability.
   (b) Clear data segregation, stringent access control, and protection against co-mingling in multi-tenancy environments.
   (c) Annual independent cyber security audit of CSP; annual review of CSP performance and resilience by Board IT sub-committee.
27. Other Security Measures:
   (a) Mandatory Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for all electronic payment debit transactions and cash withdrawals.
   (b) Robust, centralized, secure authentication preventing unauthorized/spoofed transactions.
   (c) Real-time / near real-time fraud monitoring solution to identify suspicious transactional behaviour and alert.
   (d) 24x7x365 manned facility for swift resolution of fraudulent transactions reported by customers and prompt response to Law Enforcement Agencies (LEAs).
   (e) Systematic audit log capture, analysis, storage, and archiving; unique user identification; 5-year log preservation.
   (f) Scalable payment architecture commensurate with volume, reviewed by Board sub-committee.
   (g) Secure email and messaging systems for inbound/outbound communication.
   (h) Subscription to anti-phishing / anti-rogue app takedown services.
   (i) Public awareness campaigns on fraud prevention and cyber threats.

SECTION IV – DIGITAL PAYMENT SECURITY CONTROLS
28. Online Transaction Alerts:
   Real-time online alerts based on: failed transactions, velocity, new account parameters, time zone, geolocation, IP address origin (prohibited zones / rogue IPs), behavioural biometrics, point of compromise, mobile wallets/VPAs with recorded fraud, declined transactions, transactions with no approval code.
29. Customer Notification Standards:
   (a) Mandatory masking/redaction of bank account numbers, card numbers, and confidential info in SMS/email alerts.
   (b) Online payments must display merchant name (not payment gateway/aggregator) and amount; fund transfers must display beneficiary name from account-maintaining entity.
   (c) OTP must be mentioned at the end of notification message, explicitly referencing the specific transaction.
30. In-App Fraudulent Transaction Marking:
   Customer facility on mobile app/website with authentication to identify/mark a transaction as fraudulent for seamless immediate notification to payment instrument issuer.
31. Mobile Payments Security:
   (a) Anomaly and exception detection mechanisms ensuring app functions as programmed.
   (b) Authenticated session and encryption intact throughout interaction; immediate session termination on interference or app closure with transaction resolution.
   (c) Device binding / fingerprinting with hardware, software, and SIM; re-binding required if unused for specified policy period.
   (d) Automatic termination of online session after fixed period of inactivity, prompting re-login.
   (e) Maximum failed authentication attempts threshold after which mobile access is locked; secure re-activation; immediate failure notification.
   (f) Control mechanism detecting presence of remote access applications (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, QuickSupport) and prohibiting mobile payment access while remote access is live.
   (g) Mandatory 12-hour cooling period following any change in registered mobile number or email ID before permitting online payment transactions.
32. Card Payments Security:
   (a) Merchant card-capturing terminals validated against PCI-P2PE program.
   (b) PoS terminals with PIN entry approved by PCI-PTS program.
33. Card Networks Governance:
   (a) Mandatory switch-level transaction limits at card, BIN, and card issuer levels.
   (b) 24x7x365 alert mechanism triggered to card issuer upon suspicious incidents.
   (c) Customer card details stored in encrypted form at all server locations; processing in readable format done securely.
34. Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs):
   (a) Communication of OTP and transaction alerts in vernacular languages of user's choice.
   (b) Mandatory suitable cooling period for funds transfer and cash withdrawal following electronic loading of funds on PPI.
